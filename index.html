What this app refuses

Habit apps keep score. Scripture keeps a rule.

Rings that shame. Streaks that lie. Apps that count a day you forgot to log as a day you failed. Remade was built by refusing them, one by one.

Streaks. Rings. Grades. Kept. Missed. Unknown.

Three honest words are all a ledger needs. A day you did not log is unknown, and the app never pretends it knows better. A miss is recorded only when you say so, and it is reported quietly, like a friend would.

The practice

The oldest habit system in the church.

Since the fourth century, Christians have kept a Rule of Life: a short, written pattern of daily practices. Prayer at certain hours, Scripture on certain days, rest on one. Not a program to finish. A shape to live inside.

The pattern is not the point. It exists to hold you steady in the places where God grows you. You keep the rhythm. The growing is not your job.

Remade puts that written rule in your pocket, with an honest record of how it is going.

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away. Behold, the new has come."

The honest ledger

Numbers that refuse to flatter you.

Try it. This one is real.

Tap the rows. Watch what the math does, and notice what it will not do: adherence only counts days you actually logged, coverage tells you how much of the week that was, and a habit with no entries shows nothing at all, never a fabricated zero.

Editing a habit never rewrites its history. The past keeps the rule it was lived under.

The Bible

A full Bible, not a link to one.

Every book of the Berean Standard Bible lives on your phone after one download. No connection and no account needed to read it, on a plane or at 6am before the house wakes up. It is public domain, so nothing about reading it ever costs anything.

Your identity statements draw straight from it. Scripture speaks before any habit is asked of you, because the words come first, every day, in the same order.

The shield

A pause before the apps that pull you away.

Choose the apps that eat your morning. Remade shields them until today's habits are answered, kept or honestly missed. Answering honestly opens the door exactly the way keeping the habit does. It is a pause built to ask you to be honest before you scroll, not a lock built to punish you.

Identity first

Habits are votes. Speak for who you are first.

Speak who you are

Every day opens the same way: Scripture and your own written statements of who you are in Christ, spoken before any habit is asked of you. You rehearse who you already are; the habits are votes for that person, not a score to defend.

"I am a new creation. Today I practice like one."

Commit and close

Named commitments with due dates, evidence of change observed by real people, and reviews on a weekly, monthly, and quarterly rhythm. One tap logs a day from the lock screen, a widget, or the reminder itself, so the rep costs seconds, not willpower.

"The next book opens when the rhythm is running, not when the calendar says so."
Privacy & price

Most apps promise not to look at your prayers. Remade is built so there is nothing to look at.

Free for 7 days. After that, everything you already made keeps working, for as long as you keep the app. Making more than three of any one thing costs one payment of $9.99. Not a subscription, not ever.

No account and no server. Nothing is ever transmitted to us. There is nowhere for it to go.

App Lock is on by default. Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode, from the moment setup ends.

Your free text stays home. Prayers and notes are stripped from the app's backup file in code, not in a promise. The only way they leave is a passphrase-sealed copy you make yourself.

Backups you can read. A plain file in your own iCloud Drive, under your own Apple account.

No analytics, no ads, no tracking SDKs. The app's own diagnostics never leave your phone.

The whole policy is in the app, in plain English, including what your own phone does with its own backup.
